Quick Verification of Quadrature Calibration

Before reviewing the validation benchmarks below, you can verify how calibrate_dgp() aligns population-averaged (quad_prob) and zero-random-effect (conditional_prob) cell accuracies in a crossed binary GLMM:

library(glmmplanr)

des <- design_crossed(
  factors = list(exposure = c("incidental", "intentional")),
  allocation = "latin_square",
  contrast = "sum"
)

dgp <- calibrate_dgp(
  des,
  cell_probs = c(incidental = 0.61, intentional = 0.69),
  chance = 1 / 3,
  subj_sd = 0.8,
  item_sd = 0.6
)

check_dgp(dgp, plot = FALSE)$cell_diagnostics[, c(
  "cell", "target_prob", "quad_prob", "conditional_prob"
)]
#>          cell target_prob quad_prob conditional_prob
#> 1  incidental        0.61      0.61        0.5984846
#> 2 intentional        0.69      0.69        0.6948720

Built-in Estimation and External Checks

The default planning engines are bayes_laplace for crossed binary GLMM effects, cpp_laplace for random-item Rasch recovery, and mml for fixed-item Rasch banks. Fixed-bank Rasch has fixed item intercepts and a random person intercept; its one-dimensional quadrature MML solver is a separate numerical path from crossed-GLMM Laplace estimation. A correction or validation of one path does not establish the accuracy of the other.

TAM and mirt are the primary external references for fixed-bank IRT checks. Match the response matrix, latent scale, discrimination and variance constraints, and failure denominators. Crossed random-item effects require a matching mixed model for external comparison. Previous binary special-case comparisons with mfrmr remain supporting evidence; its main application is many-facet Rasch measurement. Existing results keep their recorded engines. To reproduce earlier random-item planning with the previous default, specify engine = "lme4" explicitly.

The native covariance-update correction changes the zero-correlation, zero-guessing crossed-GLMM path. Earlier GLMM benchmarks below retain their original implementation and should not be read as a fresh validation of every old design under the correction. Fixed-bank MML is unchanged by this update and retains its existing calibration evidence.

Current numerical diagnostics. Native covariance-score stopping is implemented for zero-guessing, uncorrelated fits. Other paths retain fast approximate moment updates; their convergence does not certify stationarity of the same marginal covariance objective. Specifications, printed optimization results and plan reports identify those paths. An uncomputed covariance score is NA, including fits with covariance held fixed. Native likelihoods are also NA unless the fit converges and the final, unclamped observed curvature is positive definite. Curvature clipping may aid intermediate search steps, but cannot certify a conditional mode or supply its final Laplace determinant. A small Fisher step at a saddle is rejected. Do not use intermediate values from failed fits as objective evaluations. These checks do not certify a global mode for the nonconcave guessing-adjusted model or nominal interval calibration.

The curvature guard rejects a deterministic four-person/two-item 3AFC saddle that previously returned a finite likelihood. On the saved medium-study responses, all 2,048 native final/coarse fits and 84 selected profiles remain valid with unchanged point estimates, SEs and decisions relative to the preceding roundoff repair. This is a numerical regression check, not a new independent assessment of inference.

The conditional-mode line search now accounts for floating-point roundoff only when a small Newton step has predicted improvement below that scale. It then rechecks the original 2e-7 mode tolerance; neither this tolerance nor the iteration budget is relaxed. The study below used the preceding source; its raw records retain the old zero diagnostic sentinel and failed returns. The same-response repair replay is recorded separately in the validation suite. All 69 previously failed diagnostic optimizations recovered under the same acceptance rules, and 15 selected valid controls remained valid. Native replays of all 2,048 responses changed no coverage, detection or width decision. Three fits exceeded the predeclared 1e-4pp numerical comparison tolerance: maximum point/SE changes were .000222/.000381pp. Independent likelihood and mode checks passed for those exceptions; they are not reported as bitwise identical fits. This change does not turn the moment updates into exact joint MAP estimation or introduce a new public estimator. The selected repair replay is not an independent confirmation of nominal inference.

Planning and Input Contracts

Random-slope sensitivity is skipped when all participant, item and testlet slope SDs are zero. Multiplying zero by the sensitivity factors would repeat the same DGP and turn Monte Carlo noise into apparent sensitivity.

With mc_adjustment = "bonferroni", adjusted Wilson decisions govern whether to evaluate larger candidates or run the requested top-up. Adding verified candidates updates earlier decisions. Multi-scenario searches select a common candidate set before a fresh confirmation of every candidate-by-scenario pair; this also fixes the multiplicity used for that confirmation. Search draws are not reused as confirmation. This can add computation relative to earlier versions. Wilson coverage remains approximate, and these changes do not make an adaptively searched design a guarantee of nominal model-based inference.

A single-cell probability or positive-weight mean probability has no between-condition zero-difference null. Use criterion = "ci_width" (or an explicit ROPE). Its detection-power and joint detection/width fields are NA; coverage and precision remain meaningful. Reports distinguish accuracy from an accuracy difference, rather than interpreting an interval above 0% as power.

Custom data_fn responses must use positive integer subject, item and cell IDs within the schedule. For testlets, omitted passage IDs are recovered from the scheduled item mapping; supplied labels/IDs must agree with it. Fixed-bank MML requires unique person-item rows and nonconstant responses for items marked estimable. Public fixed-bank planning still diagnoses constant/unobserved items and retains them as failures of a full-bank precision requirement.

Native GLMM polishing and derivative calculations require unmodified observed curvature. Failed checks return non_pd_hessian, converged = FALSE and logLik = NA; ordinary iteration nonconvergence remains a separate diagnosis. The additional repair leaves all 2,048 saved native fits and 84 selected profiles numerically unchanged from the preceding curvature repair (point/SE differences zero, no decision changes). This is a regression check, not new evidence of nominal coverage or null rejection. Details and reproduction are in the repository’s validation/README.md, section planning-contract-repair.

Focused Check of Covariance Paths

A paired study used a single two-level experimental factor in 16 simulation conditions with 128 datasets each: complete 32-person by 24-item presentation or partial presentation to 72 people over 60 items, with 30 per person, guessing floor 0 or 1/3, random-effect correlation 0 or .2, and marginal contrasts 0 or 10pp from baseline .55. Participant intercept/slope SDs were .8/.35; item SDs .6/.4. Fixed Latin-square schedules used seed 37. This is a limited DGP comparison, not an estimate of an optimal required sample size.

All 2,048 native fits were valid. A diagnostic optimizer of the same Laplace objective, with full profile covariance propagation, supplied 1,979 valid intervals. It shares the native likelihood evaluator and is not an independent reference or a public engine. Numerical checks found small log-SD scores on the corrected zero-guessing/zero-correlation path; all 1,521 evaluable scores on the other paths exceeded the diagnostic .01 threshold. Fifteen additional scores could not be evaluated because of failed perturbations. In that historical run, a zero returned covariance score on those native paths denoted a bypassed check. The current implementation returns NA.

Among common valid pairs, noncanonical-path effect changes had cell-specific RMS .032–.120pp, maximum .538pp, and RMS-SE ratios .985–1.004. Eight of 1,979 pairs changed joint detection/width classification. Native coverage ranged 93.75–98.44%; null rejection ranged 3.13–6.25%, with all eight pointwise MC intervals including .05. At 128 datasets, MCSE near 5% is 1.93pp, so these results cannot establish exact nominal calibration. Failed reference fits remain unmet in all-trial detection/attainment rates; valid-only comparisons do not remove their limitations.

For a 10pp effect and width <=20pp, the complete layout had three failed planning checks and one indeterminate check; the larger partial layout passed all four. The diagnostic procedure gave the same categories despite its failures. Counts, sample sizes and total exposure change together, so this does not demonstrate that partial presentation itself improves precision. The study did not test the default bootstrap intervals or justify switching defaults. Numerical stationarity, successful fitting and inferential accuracy remain distinct requirements. Detailed scripts, responses, failures and MC intervals are retained in the repository’s validation suite.

Reading and Customizing Simulation Figures

Static plots use ggplot2::theme_bw() with automatic titles, subtitles and captions hidden by default. This styling choice follows the package user’s preference; the papers below do not prescribe this ggplot theme. Axis names, legends, scenario panels, interval bars and diagnostic counts remain visible. show_annotations = TRUE restores the generated explanations in GLMM/Rasch plot() methods and check_dgp(). Add ggplot2::labs() for custom text. The optional 3D display uses a white background and no default title; customize it with plotly::layout().

Morris, White and Crowther (2019) organize simulation reporting by estimand, generating mechanism, method and performance measure, and show performance estimates with Monte Carlo uncertainty. Accordingly, our validation and scenario figures retain their stored Monte Carlo intervals and separate rates from interval widths. These bars quantify simulation uncertainty in a performance estimate; they are not the analysis intervals whose coverage is being evaluated.

Rücker and Schwarzer (2014) use nested-loop and Trellis displays to organize many simulation conditions. Their work informs our emphasis on explicit scenario labels and panels. The current API retains small-multiple comparisons of selected designs; it does not implement their nested-loop method or imply that a few selected designs represent an entire parameter grid.

Gasparini’s rsimsum (2018) and its visualization documentation provide related examples of performance plots grouped by method and generating conditions. These are presentation references, not new estimator benchmarks or dependencies. There is no new simulation performance claim.

When exporting an unannotated figure, describe the DGP, sample/exposure schedule, replication count, interval level and failure denominator in the surrounding text or manuscript caption. Coverage and mean widths use valid fits; detection denominators follow the selected failure policy. Mean-width panels do not gain an uncertainty interval when none was stored. Plotting does not change simulated outcomes, denominators or pass/indeterminate/fail decisions. The selected marker on a contour or Pareto plot can represent an indeterminate or failed candidate: consult summary()/report_plan() or enable annotations for its status before describing it as recommended.

This is a worked planning example, not validation of an actual short form.

Native fixed-item MML evaluated 30 scenarios: five layouts (the four forms plus 150 items with 30 per person), N=120/240/480, and person SD=0.4/0.8, each with 128 replications. At SD=0.8 and N=120, the central 30 passed in 113/128 runs (88.3%, Wilson 95% interval 81.6–92.8%); the spread 30 passed in 53/128 (41.4%, 33.2–50.1%). The same central form had worse measurement at ability 3. Ease of calibration therefore cannot substitute for short-form measurement evidence. At N=480, all four fixed forms passed the assurance criterion for both SDs; the rotating 150-item pool met the joint tolerance only 1/128 times at each SD. Its mean exposure was 96 per item, compared with 480 for a fixed form. Requiring all 150 items to meet the maximum-error criterion also makes the joint target more demanding than for 30 items.

Experimental stimuli. A separate native crossed-GLMM pilot used N=60/120, J=30/60/150 and 30 items per person in a two-condition Latin square. The marginal planned difference was 8 percentage points (50% versus 58%), with person/item intercept SDs 0.8/0.6. Slope SDs were either zero or 0.25/0.35, with zero correlations and a matching analysis. Each of 12 layouts/profiles had 128 planned and 128 null runs; all 3,072 fits were valid. For N=60 and heterogeneous slopes, detection was 82.8%, 89.1%, and 85.2% at J=30,60,150, with overlapping Wilson intervals. This does not establish an optimal J. Across the pilot, null detection ranged from 1.6% to 8.6%; 128 replications do not tightly establish Type I error control. New items were sampled each replication: these results do not estimate the effect of selecting particular word identities or changing the target population to high-frequency words.

This example uses existing APIs. Content-constrained assembly can supply candidate forms, for example through TestDesign. glmmplanr evaluates their design consequences. The matched extension below now includes mlpwr and irtsim, with shared objectives, candidate designs, failure denominators and independent confirmation. The two comparisons address estimation and design search separately.

Scripts, parameters, item IDs, full curves, trial-level plan objects and Monte Carlo intervals are recorded in the repository’s item-pool validation. The macOS pilot took about 11 seconds for recalibration and 80 seconds for the experimental planned/null checks, excluding installation and compilation; these are observed times for these settings, not runtime guarantees.

Matched Comparisons with irtsim and mlpwr

The extension asks whether another Rasch workflow supports the same recalibration plans, and whether another search algorithm identifies useful designs for the same native estimator. It uses the synthetic pool and content quotas above, without adding a public API.

irtsim 0.2.0. Its public 1PL workflow with mirt and native MML evaluates identical response matrices, item IDs, masks, discrimination 1 and a mean-zero, free-variance ability scale. Five layouts, two ability SDs and five sample sizes (120/240/480/960/1920) give 50 conditions ×128 replications. Both estimators have 6,095/6,400 complete valid banks. Only two per-trial joint decisions differ, one in each direction at the .60 maximum-error boundary. All 50 attainment counts and ten proposed sample sizes agree. Item estimates are not identical (maximum paired difference .02620 logits). The screening figure shows the layout effect; it is not evidence of equivalence outside these Rasch conditions.

Independent confirmation used 15 distinct returned points ×512 fresh replications with both IRT engines. All 15 decision classifications agree (eight per-trial differences among 7,680 pairs). Of the ten minimum-N screen proposals, nine pass and one remains indeterminate: spread-60, SD=.4, N=240 reaches 80.7% (95% interval 77.0–83.9%). A separately proposed N=480 passes at 98.8% (97.5–99.5%). Rotating 150 items with 30 per person requires the tested N=1920 candidate to pass: 93.8% (91.3–95.5%) at SD=.4 and 95.1% (92.9–96.7%) at SD=.8. These are tested candidates under specified whole-bank tolerances, not universal minimum sample sizes.

mlpwr 1.1.1. It receives the same native success function, candidate grid and cost as glmmplanr’s uniform grid evaluation. This includes Rasch recovery as well as GLMM detection/precision; mlpwr already supports custom IRT simulations, as illustrated in its IRT tutorial. This comparison does not evaluate glmmplanr’s GP optimizer. There are 50 Rasch searches and 20 GLMM searches per method, using five search seeds per family/preference. Actual simulation budgets match at 30,000 Rasch and 20,880 GLMM evaluations per method. Errors, warnings and unqualified fallback coordinates remain in the report.

For the GLMM search, both methods selected independently confirmed designs in 19/20 runs. The grid’s other candidate (zero slopes, 30×30) is indeterminate; mlpwr’s other run encountered a reproducible duplicate-index error after 360 evaluations. With heterogeneous slopes, 60×30 attained the joint detection/20pp-width target in 428/512 fresh trials (83.6%, Wilson 95% interval 80.1–86.5%). Its null rejection was 29/512 (5.7%, 4.0–8.0%); passing the planning target does not guarantee nominal Type I error control. All confirmation intervals met the 20pp width limit, so detection drives the joint attainment differences in this example.

In ten rotating-pool searches, mlpwr’s default logistic surrogate warned that no good design was found. Replacing only that surrogate with a GP, using the same saved 600 outcomes and no additional simulations, returned N=1920 in all ten. This is an exploratory refit after a default-setting failure, not a prespecified GP search benchmark. It illustrates why a particular surrogate’s failure is not a general limitation of mlpwr. Both returned N=1920 designs pass the independent confirmation.

Empirical Vocabulary Banks and Replication Planning

An additional pilot uses Hamada et al.’s public vocabulary data: 1,220 participants, 50 word identities, and five booklets with 20 recall and 30 recognition responses per person. Separate 2PL fits follow the authors’ model calls. Separate Rasch working-model refits supply the native simulation banks; they are not the source paper’s reported model. Their native/mirt item differences are below .001 logits. The two formats retain their own scales.

The native pilot has 240 conditions ×64 replications, crossing two formats, five 25/50-item layouts (including the original booklets), six ability profiles and N=100/200/500/1000. A 24-condition subset has 32 paired mirt Rasch and 32 separate mirt 2PL runs. Ability shifts are sensitivity assumptions, not age or CEFR labels. All failures and constant-item banks remain misses.

For the recall Rasch bank with all 50 items and N=1000, difficulty attainment (RMSE <=.35 and maximum error <=.60) falls from 60/64 (93.8%, Wilson 95% interval 85.0–97.5%) in the reference distribution to 4/64 (6.3%, 2.5–15.0%) when its mean is shifted -3 logits, and 0/64 (0–5.7%) at -5 logits. The latter has 4.73 constant items on average. For the easier recognition bank, the -3 shift improves attainment from 24/64 (37.5%) to 61/64 (95.3%) by reducing ceiling responses. Thus the relevant quantity is the population/item match, not simply low ability. The targeting figure shows these within-format comparisons.

These outcomes concern different uses of the estimates. This pilot does not add native 2PL fitting, validate person-score precision, propagate calibration-parameter uncertainty, or establish a universal required sample size.

Native Covariance Updates and Interval Calibration

The learning example exposed a practical distinction: a design can achieve the requested detection and width targets while its nominal interval under-covers. Saved trials were used to investigate this before expanding the design grid. Fixing variance components at their generating values was a diagnostic comparison only, not an estimator available in practice.

For zero assumed random-effect correlations and no guessing lower asymptote, the covariance update now includes movement of the conditional random-effect mode in the Laplace determinant. Fixed effects and variances target the same approximate marginal objective. A small parameter change alone is no longer enough to declare convergence; the scaled covariance score must also be small. An iteration-limit failure remains a failure in planning denominators, even when finite coefficients can be returned. The correction applies to native regularized and unpenalized crossed fits, including testlets. It does not change fixed-bank MML or the correlated/guessing-adjusted calculation paths.

Screening uses the same numerical settings as confirmation on the corrected path. Its former smaller iteration budget could reject a dataset that fitted successfully in confirmation and thereby underestimate a design’s attainment. Screening remains cheaper through fewer simulation replications and omission of bootstrap refitting; a smaller numerical budget is not used to obtain that saving on this path.

Strong contrasts are also checked against a separate calculation of the fixed-effect score. Large coefficient updates continue in bounded steps. For example, a simulated sample with about 10% versus 91% correct gives an estimated marginal difference of 80.9pp. A fully separated stress example still reaches the iteration limit and remains invalid for planning. A prior permits finite coefficient estimates without guaranteeing convergence within the numerical budget; finite coefficients alone do not establish a valid fit.

Independent profile optimization, a separate conditional Hessian calculation, and finite-difference scores check the numerical change. They establish agreement with the same approximate objective, not exact integration or nominal frequentist coverage. The follow-up compares six selected layouts on fresh, matched responses; it does not identify new minimum sample sizes over the full original grid. Planning PASS and inferential diagnostics should continue to be read separately.

Scope Across Research Designs and Units of Assignment

In experiments and assessments where multiple participants respond (0 / 1) to a shared pool of items, experimental conditions can be assigned within or between participants and within or between items, with either complete or partial item presentation per participant. Both accuracy in psycholinguistic / cognitive tasks and binary preference judgments in behavioral experiments fall within this crossed binary structure. By contrast, when interventions are assigned to intact schools or clinics, the number of clusters and cluster size form distinct hierarchical design axes. Cluster-randomized school trials, multi-rater scoring allocations, and longitudinal within-person time series with serial dependence are outside the scope of glmmplanr’s built-in sample-size optimizers; probit models are covered via external-estimator comparisons.

Generating-Model Assumptions and Custom Misspecification Testing

Built-in simulation and calibration (calibrate_dgp(), simulate_dgp_data(), optimize_sample_size()) rely on the following structural assumptions:

  1. Multivariate normal random effects: Participant and item random intercepts and slopes are drawn from zero-mean multivariate normal distributions (MASS/Cholesky draws). Heavy-tailed, skewed, or mixture ability/difficulty distributions are not generated by the built-in DGP.
  2. Shared guessing floor (chance) and zero upper lapse: All items share one fixed lower asymptote \(g = \text{chance}\) and an upper asymptote of \(1.0\) (no item-specific guessing parameters, no 2PL/3PL discrimination heterogeneity, and no attentional lapses).
  3. Missingness independent of ability and responses (MCAR): Trial-level missingness (attrition), whole-participant dropout (participant_dropout), and partial item assignment (items_per_subject) are sampled independently of latent random effects and trial outcomes. Ability-dependent dropout (MNAR) or adaptive routing is outside the built-in DGP.
  4. Scope of scenario_dgps() and custom data_fn testing: Multi-scenario planning varies effect sizes, variance components, or missingness rates within this same parametric family. Passing across multiple scenarios does not guarantee robustness when the structural assumptions above are violated. To stress-test structural misspecification (e.g., ability-dependent dropout or item-specific guessing) directly under your planned analysis model, pass a custom data_fn(schedule, spec) function to evaluate_design_point() or validate_design_point():
# Example: evaluate robustness to ability-dependent (MNAR) dropout via data_fn
mnar_dropout <- function(schedule, spec) {
  df <- simulate_dgp_data(spec$dgp, schedule = schedule)
  subj_acc <- tapply(df$y, df$subj_id, mean)
  keep <- as.integer(names(subj_acc)[subj_acc >= stats::quantile(subj_acc, 0.20)])
  df[df$subj_id %in% keep, , drop = FALSE]
}

Sparse responses and the limits of a converged fit

Check the number of observed responses for each learner and each item. A correct or incorrect response contributes even when it is the only one recorded for that unit. In a three-choice task at chance, five independent responses are all wrong with probability \((2/3)^5 = 0.132\). With 32 responses the probability is about 0.00000232. These are upper bounds under the fitted guessing model with conditionally independent trials. A mechanism that forces a particular item to be wrong on every presentation would fall outside that model.

The ceiling and the guessing floor are two different limits. Write the conditional correct probability as \(p=g+(1-g)s\), with \(g=1/m\) and \(s=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\eta)\). The contribution of one binary response to expected information about \(\eta\) is \([(1-g)s(1-s)]^2/[p(1-p)]\). This approaches zero near either limit. Few responses per learner, few per item, or little within-unit variation in condition can leave variances and slope correlations weakly determined. First-order Laplace approximation uses local curvature, and increasing the total trial count while keeping each unit sparse can leave appreciable approximation error. Its direction and size depend on the design. Joe (2008)

Alignment Checklist When Comparing Sample-Size Tools

  1. Marginal vs. Conditional Input Probabilities. Under a canonical logit GLMM with a \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1^2)\) random intercept, setting \(\beta_0 = \text{qlogis}(0.80) = 1.3863\) yields \(80\%\) accuracy at zero random effect (conditional_prob) but shrinks the population-averaged accuracy (quad_prob) to \(76.13\%\); achieving an \(80\%\) population-averaged rate requires \(\beta_0 = 1.6497\). Always verify simulated cell means when passing nominal percentages across packages.
  2. Success Criteria and Failure Denominators. Hypothesis-test power, mean CI width, CI-width assurance (\(\Pr(\text{width} \le w)\)), and empirical interval coverage answer distinct questions (Pargent et al., 2024). Align link functions, contrast coding, interval estimators, and whether non-convergent replications remain in the denominator (see 3AFC comparison above).
  3. Target Item Population. Calibrating a fixed 16-word list keeps item difficulties fixed across replications (plan_rasch_fixed_bank()), whereas generalizing to new lexical samples redraws item difficulties on every replication (plan_rasch_precision(); Dunn, 2024). Report both bank-average RMSE and all-item worst-case attainment.

Passing Calibrated Marginal Probabilities to PowRPriori and simr

Suppose we plan for population-averaged cell accuracies of \(72\%\) vs. \(80\%\) with participant and item random-intercept SDs of 0.8 and 0.6 logit (chance = 0, treatment coding). In check_dgp(), quad_prob is the population-averaged accuracy across new participants and items, whereas conditional_prob is the accuracy when all random effects equal zero (75.65% vs. 83.89% after quadrature calibration):

library(glmmplanr)
design <- design_crossed(list(group = c("A", "B")), allocation = "between_subj",
                         contrast = "treatment")
dgp <- calibrate_dgp(design, cell_probs = c(A = 0.72, B = 0.80),
                     subj_sd = 0.8, item_sd = 0.6, slope_sd_ratio = 0, corr_structure = 0)
cells <- check_dgp(dgp, plot = FALSE)$cell_diagnostics
cells[c("cell", "target_prob", "quad_prob", "conditional_prob")]

b <- PowRPriori::fixed_effects_from_average_outcome(
  y ~ group, data.frame(group = factor(cells$cell, levels = c("A", "B")),
                        y = cells$conditional_prob),
  family = "binomial", center = FALSE
)

Passing cells$conditional_prob yields intercept 1.13345 and group contrast 0.51625, which can be supplied to simr::makeGlmer(fixef = unlist(b), ...) alongside random-intercept variances 0.64 and 0.36. By contrast, transforming 0.72 and 0.80 directly via qlogis() yields slope 0.44183 and shrinks the true population-averaged cell probabilities to 68.68% vs. 76.13% (a 7.45 pp rather than 8.00 pp marginal difference; see PowRPriori workflow and simr from-scratch vignette).

Across 192 datasets on a complete \(64 \times 24\) schedule (1,536 observations, two-sided 5% Wald test), calibrating the 8-percentage-point marginal difference produced 188/192 (97.9%) detections for both glmmplanr and simr under a within-participant Latin square, and 115/192 (59.9%, glmmplanr), 111/192 (57.8%, simr on identical responses), and 112/192 (58.3%, independent PowRPriori::power_sim()) under between-participant assignment (32 vs. 32). Without quadrature calibration, between-participant detections dropped to 95, 92, and 96 of 192, illustrating how conflating marginal and conditional probabilities alters both the simulated population and resulting power estimates.
